# Phase 01 — UX Research Plan

**Artifact state:** DRAFT_FOR_REVIEW — REQUIRES HG-01 APPROVAL  
**Research status:** No interviews, observations, surveys, or usability tests have occurred.  
**Data rule:** Use fictional financial data and synthetic scenarios only.

## Documented facts

- The BRD proposes an optional Fin Buddy AI assistant.
- Must-use cases are spending summaries, budget Q&A, transaction search, and financial education.
- Consent, grounded answers, deterministic calculations, inspectable evidence, correction, privacy, and accessibility are requirements.
- Autonomous payments, investing, credit decisions, and unsupported real-time claims are outside MVP scope.
- The four user-problem statements remain hypotheses, not findings.

## Assumptions to test

1. Users struggle to explain spending changes.
2. Users prefer direct conversational answers to navigating dashboards.
3. Users need contextual assistance for savings decisions.
4. Visible evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations increase trust.
5. Proposed target segments experience sufficiently similar problems for one MVP.
6. Users will grant scoped access when value and controls are understandable.

## Proposed participants

Eight moderated interviews of 45–60 minutes. This is directional qualitative research, not statistically representative.

Include adults who manage recurring income/expenses, use a financial tracking method at least monthly, and recently reviewed spending, a budget, a transaction, or a savings goal. Seek variation in budgeting behavior, savings goals, financial confidence, household responsibility, app familiarity, AI trust, language, and accessibility needs.

Exclude project stakeholders, participants unable to consent, and recruitment specifically targeting people in acute financial crisis. Financial professionals may be a separate expert stream, not primary users.

## Recruitment and privacy constraints

- Confirm market, language, incentives, recruitment channel, schedule, and accommodations before outreach.
- Do not request balances, account numbers, credentials, exports, or real account screenshots.
- Obtain informed consent and separate recording consent.
- Allow skipping or withdrawal without loss of incentive.
- Use participant IDs; store contact data separately from notes.
- Redact incidental personal or financial information immediately.
- Restrict raw material to approved research personnel and approved storage.
- Do not upload recordings or identifiable notes to AI services without separate privacy approval and participant consent.
- Define and communicate retention and deletion dates before research begins.

## Interview guide

### Current behavior

1. Tell me how you currently keep track of everyday money.
2. Walk me through the last time you reviewed your spending.
3. What were you trying to understand, and what was easy or difficult?
4. Tell me about a recent spending total or category that surprised you.
5. How did you investigate it, and what affected your confidence?
6. Tell me about the last time you searched for a transaction or recurring charge.

### Budgets and savings

7. How, if at all, do you decide how much you can spend in a category?
8. Tell me about a recent time you changed or exceeded a budget.
9. If you have a savings goal, how do you decide what to contribute?
10. What makes a savings plan feel realistic or unrealistic?

### Assistance and trust

11. When you have a money question, where do you go for help?
12. If you have used AI for a money question, walk me through the experience.
13. What would make you question an automated financial answer?
14. What would you need to inspect before relying on an explanation?
15. How would you distinguish helpful education from unwanted advice?

### Synthetic scenario probes

16. A fictional app says, “You spent ₹4,200 more this month.” What would you want to know next?
17. What would help you verify the statement?
18. If a fictional transaction is miscategorized, what would you expect to do?
19. What should be explained before an app uses fictional transactions and budgets to answer a question?
20. What response is useful when there is not enough data?
21. Which controls do you expect for chat history, permission withdrawal, and deletion?
22. Is there anything a financial assistant should never do?

## Analysis plan

1. Prepare redacted notes using participant IDs.
2. Separate behavior, stated preference, interpretation, and assumption.
3. Code around spending comprehension, navigation, budgeting, savings, trust, consent, correction, accessibility, and advice boundaries.
4. Map observations to H1–H4 and BR-01–BR-09.
5. Retain contradictions and negative cases.
6. Compare patterns across meaningful participant variations.
7. Report evidence, counterevidence, limitations, confidence, and implications separately.
8. Create personas only if behavioral patterns emerge after research.

## Evidence thresholds

- Strong directional pattern: recent behavioral evidence from at least five of eight participants across two meaningful variations, without severe contradiction.
- Moderate pattern: evidence from three or four participants or a clear subgroup.
- Weak signal: one or two participants or hypothetical preference only.
- Mixed: meaningful contradiction; preserve both views and investigate.
- Unsupported: no participant evidence; retain as an assumption.
- Critical safety/privacy issue: log and assess a credible severe issue even if reported once.

No hypothesis will be described as statistically validated from eight qualitative interviews.
